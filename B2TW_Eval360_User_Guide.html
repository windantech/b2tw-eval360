Chapter 1

What this is, and the problem it solves

Read this chapter even if you read nothing else.

Eval360 is the monitoring and evaluation system for B2THEWORLD's Transformative Education work. It holds the schools, the children, the teachers, the school leaders, the stakeholder training, the school self-study, the team's own culture and the money, across 5 countries — and it produces the six documents B2THEWORLD and its partners need, all from the same figures.

The problem it was built for

B2THEWORLD does not lack an M&E plan. It has three documents, and they disagree. The 2024 M&E Plan Template says 10,000 students; the LogFrame says 6,896 in one place and 6,898 in another. There are 9 such differences. Eleven sections of the plan were never filled in at all — including the performance evaluation plan, the data quality plan and the performance results report, which are the three places a reader goes to check any figure. And the newest thinking, Impact Builder POOA v5, carries no targets at all.

A reporting system that quietly picks one of those numbers is worse than no system, because the figure it picks becomes the denominator of every percentage anyone ever reports. So this one does three things instead.

  1. It holds all three documents, separately. The 2024 logframe runs the operational screens because it is the only one with targets. POOA v5 is the adopted theory of change and has its own screen. They are never merged.
  2. It shows you every difference, quoted from both sides, and records what you decide. The decision of 12 Oct 2026 — highest stated value — is on the record against B2THEWORLD leadership, and can be changed on one screen. The code never chooses.
  3. It names what is missing instead of reporting zero. Where a section of the source plan is blank, the report prints what is missing, where it is missing from, and the question that would fill it.
The one thing to take away
Every figure in this system is recorded once, in the register it belongs to, and read everywhere it is needed. Nothing is typed twice, so nothing can disagree with itself — and where the source documents disagree, you are told.

Chapter 2

Getting around

The shell, your role, and the reporting period.

Every screen has the same three parts: the sidebar on the left, grouped by what you are doing; the top bar with the screen name, the role you are viewing as, the reporting period, and the consistency-check chip; and the screen itself.

The role selector matters more than it looks

The system has 8 accounts across 6 roles. Changing the role in the top bar changes what the sidebar offers, what your inbox asks of you, and on a country role, which country's figures you see. It is not cosmetic — switch to a Country role and the funding screen becomes read-only.

1
Pick who you areTop bar, left of the period selector. Seven of the eight names come from A §5.2.
2
Pick the periodTop bar. The year has just closed, so it opens on the full year with the annual report due.
3
Watch the chipBottom left. It runs 37 internal checks — that the two plans stayed apart, that every target matches a recorded decision, that nothing blank was filled in.
Nothing is saved
This demonstration uses no browser storage and no database. State lives in memory while the tab is open; a reload returns every figure to the seed. The seed is fixed, so the figures are identical on every machine.
Chapter 3

The plan, held three ways

Theory of Change, Logframe, Indicators.

Three screens hold the plan, because three documents describe it. None of them is a summary of the others.

Theory of Change — POOA v5

The adopted thinking: three problems, three outcomes, three outputs, three activity sets and six assumptions. It is the newest document and the one B2THEWORLD intends to work to. It carries no targets, no dates and no countries, which is why it cannot run the operational screens on its own.

Logframe — the 2024 projects

Three projects, each a full logframe from Impact down to Inputs, in the client's own wording. Nothing is paraphrased. Rows the source leaves blank — B row 9, numbered "4" with nothing beside it — are shown as blank, flagged, and not filled in.

Indicators — both generations, one list

13 indicators from both plans. Each shows its target, its current value and its status — and where there is no target, it says so rather than inventing one. 2 indicators cannot be computed from anything the system holds; they report nothing, which is not the same as reporting zero.

An indicator reads its target; it never copies it
This matters. If you change a decision on the Plan Reconciliation screen, every indicator that depends on it moves in the same instant. Nothing has to be rebuilt, and nothing goes stale.
Chapter 4

Plan Reconciliation

The nine differences, quoted from both sides, with the decision on the record. This is the chapter that explains everything else.

9 differences across the three documents. For each one, this screen shows every figure that was stated, quotes the document it came from, says which is in force, and names who decided and when.

The rule in force

On 12 Oct 2026, B2THEWORLD leadership ruled: highest stated value. Where two or more source documents state a different figure for the same thing, the system uses the highest stated figure. Blanks are not filled, and goals that differ in kind rather than in size are all kept.

What that choice costs, so nobody is surprised later

Taking the highest figure makes every target the most ambitious one available — 10,000 children rather than 6,898, 500 stakeholders rather than 250. Achievement therefore reads lower than it would against the other documents: the same delivery, measured against a longer ruler. 7,963 children is 79.6% of the adopted target and 115.4% of the one not chosen. Both are shown, on every screen and in every report, so the denominator is never hidden.

Three places "highest" decides nothing

  • The goal. A, B and POOA v5 state three different goals, not three sizes of one. All three are kept, each against its own source.
  • The two strategy generations. Not a number. POOA v5 stays the theory of change; the 2024 logframe supplies the targets, because POOA v5 has none.
  • The blanks. A blank is not a contradiction. Nothing blank is filled.
One figure needed a second ruling
The 27 partner schools resolve cleanly at the headline, but the per-country breakdowns disagree beneath it. Taking the highest value cell by cell — Pakistan's 3 primary schools from A §2.2 against Output 2's none — gives 30, a figure no document states. The system uses 27 with Output 2's breakdown, and carries Pakistan's 3 as an open question rather than dropping them.

Chapter 5

The network

Countries, partner schools, children.

5 countries, 27 partner schools, 7,963 children. The country register carries each country's status, the year it joined, its coordinator, and the context that explains the work — and where a document contradicts itself about a country, the contradiction travels with it.

Schools

14 primary and 13 secondary. Six carry the names the source documents give them; the rest are held by register code, because inventing a school name would be inventing a fact. Every school has an enrolment, a teacher count, two self-study rounds and a stage.

Children

Enrolment, attendance, retention, transition and graduation, by sex and by country. Attendance runs at 90.7% against the goal of 95%.

A §2.2 asked for all of this split by sex, and left all 48 cells empty
The split this system reports is computed from the enrolment register instead. It is not what the plan asked for, and the screen says so. No child is named anywhere in this system, and no report it generates carries an individual record.
Chapter 6

People

Teachers, school leaders, stakeholder training — Project 2 and POOA v5’s OC1 and OC2.

Teachers

342 teachers, 288 trained in Why/Play/Lead (84.2%). Every teacher was observed twice on the same five-domain instrument, so the percentage increase OC1 asks for is computable rather than asserted: 2.35 to 2.93, a 24.7% increase.

Did the training work?

Trained teachers gained 0.67 on the instrument; untrained teachers gained 0.1. The difference is 0.57, between 288 trained and 54 untrained teachers on the same instrument.

What that comparison is not
It is not a controlled trial. The two groups were not assigned at random, and the untrained teachers may differ from the trained ones in ways nobody recorded. Observation is also the softest data in this system: it is a judgement, usually by someone who knows the teacher, and the system does not record who made it. All of that is said on the screen, not buried here.

School leaders

54 leaders against OC2's four transformational-leadership indicators. 19 (35.2%) apply all four. "Consistently implement" is read as all four rather than some — a choice this system made, because POOA v5 does not define it, and one the screen states.

Stakeholder training — Project 2

350 stakeholders in ongoing training of 397 enrolled, against a target of 500 from A §5.2. The other documents say 250 and 150; the highest was adopted, so this reads 70% rather than 140%.

The improvement plans

AC3 asks each school for a prioritised plan. Each school's two priorities are its own lowest-scoring domains, taken from its own assessment rather than handed to it from outside. Across the network they add to 54 priorities, landing most often on teaching and learning quality.

Safeguarding and student care

These are children affected by armed conflict, persecution and displacement. Two of the self-study domains cover it directly, and every school is scored on both.

What the source documents say about child protection
None of the three describes a child protection policy, a referral pathway or a reporting line. That is a statement about the documents, not about B2THEWORLD — the screen says so in those words, and names it as the most important gap in the plan.

The limit of what a system can see
These are observable proxies — a session attended, a book delivered, an app account posting. Whether someone is living out a rule of life is not something software can see, and this system does not pretend otherwise. B rows 67 and 72 to 79 give no means of verification for most of this project, which is carried as gap G10.

The team

A §4 asks for the M&E team and their tasks, and is empty. What the Team screen shows is reconstructed from A §5.2's "staff responsible" column — the only place any document names who does what. It is not a substitute for §4: it says who reports a figure, not who is accountable for the result, and the screen says exactly that.

The weakest measure across everything is Integrity at 42.9%: almost nothing in this system records who changed a figure or when. It is the cheapest thing to fix and the first thing a verification mission asks about.

This is the system marking its own homework
Said on the screen, in those words. The assessment is a draft of what A §7.1 would have contained, written from the registers rather than from nothing — but it is this system's draft, not B2THEWORLD's plan, and the difference matters.

The plan itself, scored

47 out of 100 — Half-settled — the differences are decided, the blanks are not. The two halves tell the story: 100% of the differences are settled (8 of 8) and 0% of the blanks are filled (0 of 11).

The score is the share of what a plan needs that this plan has, weighted by severity — not a hundred with penalties taken off it. Penalising each finding put this plan at 4 out of 100, which reads the same whether one blank is filled or ten are, and so gives nobody a reason to fill the first one.

This screen has no fix-it button, and that is deliberate
The source material says plainly: do not invent values for blanks or resolve differing targets without approval. So a difference gets a "Decide" button that takes you to the reconciliation, and a blank gets no button at all — only the sentence "Only B2THEWORLD can answer this".
Chapter 12

Ask Eval360

Computed answers, with the working attached.

Ask a question in plain words and get an answer assembled from the registers. There is no language model behind it: every answer is produced by a function reading 7 registers, which is why each one can show its working and why none of them can invent a figure.

Two things it will not do

  • It will not give you a figure the source documents disagree about without telling you they disagree.
  • It will not give you anything about an individual child. It receives counts, never records, and no child's name exists anywhere in this system to give.

Each answer ends with Where this came from — the registers and source documents behind it. Where the data will not support a strong claim, the answer says so: ask whether the teacher training worked and it gives you the trained-versus-untrained comparison, then tells you it is not a controlled trial.

Chapter 13

Users, roles and configuration

What a deployment sets, and who can see what.

8 accounts across 6 roles. A role's entry lists only its exceptions — anything not listed is full access — so adding a screen does not silently lock everyone out of it.

No role is restricted from student data, because there is no student data to restrict
Every figure in this system is a count. No account, at any level, can open an individual child's record — none exists, and none of the six reports carries one. What the roles control is which screens an account sees and whether it can change anything on them.

Configuration

The organisation, the project calendar, the rule that governs every target, the three source documents, and the brand. The brand swatches are live: change one and every screen and chart follows it, because no component in this system hardcodes a colour — they all read the custom property.

Three sentences this system will keep saying to you

  • No target set is not zero. Cannot be computed is not zero either.
  • A blank in the plan prints the question that would fill it, and keeps printing it until somebody answers.
  • Every percentage has a denominator, and the denominator was a decision — which is shown beside it.
